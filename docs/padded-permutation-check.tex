\title{The Padded Permutation Check:\\
Identity-Padded Dead Cells in the Wiring Grand Product\\[0.6ex]
\large Design note --- v1 (shipped)}
\author{Flock}
\date{August 3, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The circuit wiring layer proves its copy constraints with a grand-product
permutation check over the cell space $\Bmu$. The product-circuit GKR that
proves it is structured over the full hypercube, so the prover paid for
every cell --- live or dead --- even though a typical recursion circuit's
cell space is mostly padding. This note records the fix, inspired by how
SP1's LogUp GKR pads its fractions with the additive/multiplicative
identities $(0,1)$: redefine the fingerprint of a \emph{dead} cell to be
the multiplicative identity $1$, via a statement-derived live-set
selector. The check's soundness argument is unchanged (the same multiset
identity, restricted to the live set), \emph{no sumcheck degree or round
count changes anywhere}, the endpoint checks remain closed-form because
the witness is already zero on dead cells and the selector multiplies
only structural terms, and the prover becomes live-proportional. Measured
on the first real recursion node's outer circuit ($\mu = 24$): the wiring
prove dropped from ${\sim}46$\,ms (at the smaller $\mu = 23$) to
${\sim}27$\,ms, and the $\sigma$-aware (root-style) wiring verify from
25--47\,ms to ${\sim}5$\,ms.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and the problem}

The wiring layer (\code{circuit-wiring-design.tex}) enumerates a
\emph{cell space} $\Bmu$, $\mu = \nu + c$: cell $x = (\iota, r)$ with the
$c$ slot bits high and the $\nu$ row bits low, so cell index
$x = (\iota \ll \nu) \,|\, r$. A cell-slot $\iota$ is one schema word of
one gate type (or a $2^{\nu}$-word block of the public segment); its rows
$r < \mathrm{count}_\iota$ are \emph{live} and the tail
$r \ge \mathrm{count}_\iota$ is \emph{dead}. The witness vector
$w : \Bmu \to \F$ carries the committed word (or public word) at each
live cell and is \emph{zero on every dead cell} --- an existing invariant
(dummy rows are honest zeros; \code{CellSpace} pads with zero cells).

Copy constraints are one permutation $\sigma$ of $\Bmu$ that fixes every
dead cell, checked by the Plonk-style grand product: for random
$\alpha, \beta$ drawn after $w$ is bound,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:plain}
\prod_{x \in \Bmu}\bigl(w(x) + \alpha\,\sid(x) + \beta\bigr)
\;=\;
\prod_{x \in \Bmu}\bigl(w(x) + \alpha\,\ssig(x) + \beta\bigr),
\qquad \ssig = \sid \circ \sigma,
\end{equation}
proved by a two-product GKR (batched to a single reduction point $\rho$)
whose endpoint the verifier checks against $\widehat{w}(\rho)$ (the
gather recombination), the closed form $\widehat{\sid}(\rho)$, and the
$\sigma$ table.

The cost problem: a dead cell's factor $\alpha\,\sid(x) + \beta$ is
neither trivial nor constant, and the product-circuit GKR is a complete
binary tree over $\Bmu$ --- the prover touches every leaf and every
internal node whether or not its subtree is padding. There is no
``sparse arm'' to add to a hypercube product tree: the domain shape
\emph{is} the cost. On the first real recursion node's outer circuit,
$2^{24}$ cells carried ${\sim}400$k live ones; the wiring prover and the
$\sigma$-aware verifier both paid the $40\times$ difference.

\section{The SP1 pattern, and the masked fingerprint}

SP1's LogUp GKR solves the same problem by \emph{choosing} its padding to
be the identity of the operation being aggregated: padded numerators are
$0$, padded denominators are $1$, and each sumcheck round tracks a
closed-form \code{padding\_adjustment} instead of touching padded memory
(\code{sp1/crates/hypercube/src/logup\_gkr/logup\_poly.rs}). The padded
region contributes nothing real, so the prover's per-layer work is
proportional to the real data.

We adopt the same convention for the permutation check. Let
$\live : \Bmu \to \{0,1\}$ be the live-set indicator --- per slot, the
aligned row prefix $[0, \mathrm{count}_\iota)$. Redefine the leaves of
both product circuits as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:masked}
L(x) \;=\; \live(x)\,\bigl(w(x) + \alpha\,\sid(x) + \beta\bigr)
\;+\; \bigl(1 - \live(x)\bigr),
\end{equation}
and symmetrically with $\ssig$ on the right-hand side. A dead leaf is
exactly $1$; the products range over the \emph{live fingerprint multiset}
only.

Two facts make this sound with no new machinery:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{The mask is statement-derived.} $\live$ is a function of
the declared per-type counts and the public-segment length --- both part
of the circuit encoding whose digest is bound into the transcript before
$\alpha, \beta$ are drawn (\code{Circuit::live\_mask},
\code{CellSpace::live\_counts}). No prover freedom enters.
\item \textbf{$\sigma$ fixes dead cells} (enforced at circuit build; the
prover \code{debug\_assert}s it, together with $w$'s dead-zero
invariant). So the two sides' dead factors are the same constant $1$,
and removing them changes neither side's honest product.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[Soundness of the masked check]
Let $\sigma$ fix every dead cell. For $\alpha, \beta$ uniform in $\F$,
the masked products
$\prod_x L(x) = \prod_x L_\sigma(x)$ agree with probability
$1 - O(2^{\mu})/|\F|$ only if the live multisets agree:
\[
\bigl\{\, (w(x), \sid(x)) : x \ \mathrm{live} \,\bigr\}
\;=\;
\bigl\{\, (w(x), \ssig(x)) : x \ \mathrm{live} \,\bigr\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Dead factors are $1$ on both sides and drop from both products. What
remains is the standard argument for \eqref{eq:plain} restricted to the
live set: the two sides are polynomials in $(\alpha, \beta)$ of total
degree at most the live count, equal as polynomials iff the two multisets
of linear factors agree; Schwartz--Zippel bounds the failure probability
for random $(\alpha, \beta)$.
\end{proof}

Completeness is immediate: an honest witness satisfies the live multiset
identity, and the dead $1$s multiply to $1$.

\section{No degree changes --- and why the endpoint stays closed-form}

Two places could plausibly pay for the selector; neither does.

\paragraph{The layer sumchecks.} The GKR's round polynomials only ever
see $\eq(\cdot)\cdot V(y,0)\cdot V(y,1)$ where $V$ is the layer below's
multilinear extension --- degree $3$ per round \emph{regardless of what
the leaves are}. The leaf's algebraic form never enters a round
polynomial; masking changes leaf \emph{values} only. Round counts, proof
shape, and the verifier's per-round algebra are all unchanged.

\paragraph{The endpoint (input-layer) check.} Here the na\"ive reasoning
breaks and the structure saves us. The endpoint claim is about the MLE of
the leaf \emph{vector}, and MLEs do not commute with pointwise products:
$\widehat{\live \cdot g} \ne \lv \cdot \widehat{g}$ in general. But
expand \eqref{eq:masked} over the cube using two facts --- $w$ is already
zero on dead cells (so $\live \cdot w = w$ pointwise), and we are in
characteristic 2 (so $-1 = +1$):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:affine}
L \;=\; w \;+\; \alpha\,(\live \odot \sid) \;+\; (\beta + 1)\,\live \;+\; 1 .
\end{equation}
This is an \emph{affine combination of vectors} --- $w$, the masked tag
$\live \odot \sid$, and $\live$ --- and MLEs are linear, so at the GKR's
reduction point $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:endpoint}
\widehat{V}_{\mathrm{lhs}}(\rho)
= \widehat{w}(\rho) + \alpha\,\Mh(\rho) + (\beta + 1)\,\lv(\rho) + 1,
\qquad
\widehat{V}_{\mathrm{rhs}}(\rho)
= \widehat{w}(\rho) + \alpha\,\widehat{\live \odot \ssig}(\rho)
  + (\beta + 1)\,\lv(\rho) + 1,
\end{equation}
where $\Mh = \widehat{\live \odot \sid}$. The selector multiplies only
\emph{structural, verifier-known} functions, and both new terms have
closed forms over the aligned per-slot prefixes:

\paragraph{$\lv(\rho)$ --- the count-binding prefix $\eq$-sum.} Split
$\rho = (\rho_{\mathrm{lo}}, \rho_{\mathrm{hi}})$ at $\nu$. Then
$\lv(\rho) = \sum_\iota \eq(\rho_{\mathrm{hi}}, \iota)\,
E_{c_\iota}(\rho_{\mathrm{lo}})$ with
$E_n(p) = \sum_{i < n} \eq(p, i)$. Decompose $[0, n)$ into one aligned
subcube per set bit $t$ of $n$ (bits above $t$ equal $n$'s, bit $t$
zero, bits below free); partition of unity collapses each subcube's free
part to $1$, giving the $O(\nu)$ form
\[
E_n(p) \;=\; \sum_{t\,:\,n_t = 1} (1 + p_t) \prod_{j > t} q_j,
\qquad
q_j = \begin{cases} p_j & n_j = 1 \\ 1 + p_j & n_j = 0. \end{cases}
\]

\paragraph{$\Mh(\rho)$ --- the tag-weighted variant.} The tag is
$\Ftwo$-linear in the cell bits,
$\sid(\iota \ll \nu \,|\, r) = \mathrm{tag}_{\mathrm{hi}}(\iota) +
\mathrm{tag}_{\mathrm{lo}}(r)$, so per slot
\[
\Mh(\rho) = \sum_\iota \eq(\rho_{\mathrm{hi}}, \iota)
\Bigl[\, \mathrm{tag}_{\mathrm{hi}}(\iota)\, E_{c_\iota}(\rho_{\mathrm{lo}})
\;+\; D_{c_\iota}(\rho_{\mathrm{lo}}) \,\Bigr],
\qquad
D_n(p) = \sum_{i<n} \eq(p, i)\,\mathrm{tag}_{\mathrm{lo}}(i),
\]
and $D_n$ has the same subcube decomposition with each subcube
contributing its fixed high bits' tags plus $p_j$ per free bit ---
$O(\nu^2)$ per slot. Both evaluators are
\code{product\_gkr::LiveMask::\{live\_eval, masked\_id\_eval\}}, pinned
against explicit-table MLEs by
\code{live\_mask\_closed\_forms\_and\_masked\_roundtrip}.

The prover reconstructs $f(\rho), g(\rho)$ from the collapsed claims by
the same affine relation (char 2: subtraction is addition), so the
surfaced evaluations keep their meaning --- $f(\rho) = g(\rho) =
\widehat{w}(\rho)$ in the copy-constraint case --- and the gather
recombination check is untouched.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\sigma$}{sigma} side}

The one term with no closed form is
$\widehat{\live \odot \ssig}(\rho)$. It was already the term the design
defers (sigma route B, \code{circuit-wiring-design.tex} \S sigma): the
deferred table simply becomes the \emph{masked} table
$\live \odot \ssig$ --- \code{SigmaAssertion::matrix} zeroes dead
entries --- and the accumulator's \code{MatrixClaim} reshape, the
$2{\to}1$ folds, and the root discharge carry over with no shape change.
Since $\live$ is circuit-determined, same-circuit children still share
one table and the merge-node normalisation holds.

Better still, the masked table is \emph{sparse by definition}: dead
entries are zero. So its evaluation needs no materialisation at all:
\[
\widehat{\live \odot \ssig}(\rho)
= \sum_\iota \eq(\rho_{\mathrm{hi}}, \iota)
  \sum_{r < c_\iota} \eq(\rho_{\mathrm{lo}}, r)\; \sid(\sigma(\iota \ll \nu | r)),
\]
an $O(\mathrm{live} + 2^{\nu} + 2^{c})$ sum
(\code{sparse\_sigma\_eval}) used by the prover \emph{and} by the
$\sigma$-known verifier arm. This is what collapsed the root-style
wiring verify from 25--47\,ms to ${\sim}5$\,ms: the ``sigma v1 root
cost'' was the dense masked-table build plus a dense MLE fold, both of
which the sparsity removes.

\section{The live-proportional prover (the grouped pipeline)}

With identity padding in place, the prover can skip dead regions
structurally. The key observation:

\begin{lemma}[Fold invariance of all-ones regions]
Both primitives of the product-circuit GKR preserve constant-$1$
regions: the layer gate maps $(1, 1) \mapsto 1 \cdot 1 = 1$, and the
sumcheck fold maps $(1, 1) \mapsto 1\cdot(1{+}\rho) + 1\cdot\rho = 1$
for any $\rho$.
\end{lemma}

Because the cell index is (slot high, row low), every slot's dead tail
is an \emph{aligned suffix of an aligned block}, and the lemma means
this shape survives every pass. A layer is therefore stored in
\emph{grouped} form (\code{GVec}): per group, a live prefix in a dense
buffer over an implicit all-ones tail that is never written or read.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Leaves}: only live prefixes are computed
  ($O(\mathrm{live})$ writes).
\item \textbf{Layer build} (\code{gv\_build\_prev}): the high-bit
  pairing merges group $g$ with group $g + G/2$ row-wise; the merged
  prefix length is the \emph{max} of the pair, and the shorter side's
  ``multiplication by 1'' is a copy. Below the single-group layer the
  same rule applies to row halves ($\mathrm{len}' = \min(\mathrm{len}, h)$).
\item \textbf{Folds} (\code{gv\_fold}): prefixes halve
  ($\mathrm{len}' = \lceil \mathrm{len}/2 \rceil$); an odd boundary pair
  mixes in one explicit $1$; once groups are single entries, both-ones
  pairs stay $1$ without a write.
\item \textbf{Round messages} (\code{gv\_message}): the live pair extent
  is summed directly (branch-minimal guarded reads --- a factor beyond
  its own prefix reads as $1$). An \emph{all-ones tail pair} contributes,
  for the $\lambda$-batched two-circuit gate,
  \[
  v_1 = 1\cdot 1 + \lambda\cdot 1\cdot 1 = 1 + \lambda,
  \qquad
  v_\infty = (1{+}1)(1{+}1) + \lambda\,(1{+}1)(1{+}1) = 0,
  \]
  (char 2 kills the leading coefficient), so tails add
  $(1{+}\lambda)\cdot(\text{$\eq$-mass of the tail range})$ to $G(1)$
  and nothing to $G(\infty)$. The $\eq$-mass of any contiguous range is
  $O(1)$ from prefix-sum arrays over the split $\eq$ tables
  (\code{range\_eq\_sum}) --- our per-group analogue of SP1's
  \code{padding\_adjustment}.
\end{itemize}

Total prover work: $O(\mathrm{live})$ field operations plus
$O(\#\mathrm{groups} \cdot \mu)$ bookkeeping and the per-round $\eq$
tables. One honest caveat on the constant: full public slots are
\emph{genuinely} live, and the max-merge rule means a fully-live group
infects its merge partners as layers shrink, so mid-tree layers do not
thin as fast as the leaf census suggests. The measured residual (below)
is dominated by that real data, not by padding.

Because $\F_{2^{128}}$ addition is XOR --- exactly associative and
commutative --- regrouping the sums changes nothing: the grouped
pipeline is \emph{transcript-identical} to the dense masked one. The
dense pipeline is kept reachable
(\code{prove\_batched\_dense\_masked\_for\_tests}) and
\code{grouped\_matches\_dense\_masked} pins proof-and-claim byte
equality on random $(f, g, \sigma, \mathrm{mask})$ across several
$(\nu, c)$ shapes --- a permanent differential oracle.

\section{Verifier and in-circuit deltas}

The verifier's only changes are the endpoint formulas
\eqref{eq:endpoint} (both arms) and, on the $\sigma$-known arm, the
sparse masked evaluation. A verifier holding a wrong mask fails the
input checks (unit-pinned).

In the recursion circuit's GKR transcription, the input-check zero-deltas
consume $\Mh(\rho)$ and $\lv(\rho)$ as checker-validated advice publics
(the alpha-expansion trust tier): both are structural functions of the
published point $\rho$, the checker recomputes them from the circuit's
own \code{live\_mask}, and the pair is additionally pinned by the two
published zero-deltas given the bound claim chains. The previous
in-circuit $\sid$ basis chain ($\mu$ MAC rows and $\mu$ constant
publics) retires.

\section{Measured}

Swap-outer circuit (the first real recursion node's outer,
$\nu = 15$, $\mu = 24$, ${\sim}250$ gate cell-slots, ${\sim}400$k live
cells), M4 Max, cold box (m30 sanity probe ${\sim}117$\,ms first):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
 & dense (pre-mask) & masked + grouped \\
\midrule
wiring prove (build $w$ + GKR + gather) & ${\sim}46$\,ms at $\mu{=}23$ & ${\sim}27$\,ms at $\mu{=}24$ \\
\quad of which grouped GKR & --- & leaves 3.2 + layers 4.1 + sumchecks 19.1 \\
wiring verify, $\sigma$-known (root-style) & 25--47\,ms & ${\sim}5$\,ms \\
wiring verify, deferred (route B) & ${\sim}2$\,ms & ${\sim}2$\,ms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

No byte fixtures covered circuit-path proofs, so the transcript change
required no re-pin.
