\begin{abstract}
When ring switching \cite{dp-ringswitch} is composed with a jagged
(stacked-column) commitment \cite{jagged}, its weight and sumcheck live
on the \emph{virtual} (capacity-padded) domain, so the prover pays one
fold-map evaluation per virtual word --- proportional to provisioned
capacity, not committed data. We merge the two reductions into one
sumcheck over the dense domain, making this cost count-proportional.
The verifier's resulting obligation --- a succinct evaluation of the
jagged multilinear \emph{twisted} by the $\Ftwo$-linear fold map $\Phi$
--- is discharged by writing $\Phi$ as a linearized polynomial
$\sum_j c_j x^{2^j}$: since Frobenius commutes with the eq-product
structure at Boolean points, the twisted evaluation equals a known
combination of $128$ \emph{ordinary} jagged evaluations at
Frobenius-powered points, delegated by a single batched sumcheck of the
existing assist form with deterministic weights.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

$\F = \F_{2^{128}}$; $\eq(\tau, x) = \prod_i (\tau_i x_i +
(1{-}\tau_i)(1{-}x_i))$; hats denote multilinear extensions; $z^{2^j}$
is the coordinate-wise Frobenius power of a point $z$.

\paragraph{Ring switching.} A bit-level claim on an $\Ftwo$-valued
witness, packed $128$ bits per $\F$-word, is reduced by ring switching
to an inner-product claim over the $2^{\ell}$-word packed domain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rs}
  \textstyle\sum_{e \in \{0,1\}^{\ell}} f(e)\, b(e) = t,
  \qquad
  b(e) = \Phi\bigl(\eq(z, e)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $f$ is the packed witness, $z \in \F^{\ell}$ is the claim's
word-level point, and $\Phi : \F \to \F$ is an $\Ftwo$-linear map
determined by the claim's in-word structure and fresh verifier
randomness. We treat a single claim throughout;
Remark~\ref{rem:multi} handles several. $\Phi$ does not commute with
field multiplication, so $b$ factors as no eq tensor; the verifier can
nevertheless evaluate $\hat b$ anywhere in polylogarithmic time.

\paragraph{Jagged commitments.} A jagged layout over $2^{n}$ rows
$\times$ $2^{k}$ columns assigns column $y$ a public height $h_y$, with
prefix sums $T_y$; we assume $\sum_y h_y = 2^m$ (append a final
zero-filled padding column otherwise --- no loss of generality, and it
frees the exposition of tail cases). The commitment binds the dense
stack $q \in \F^{2^m}$; $\colof(d)$ is the unique $y$ with
$T_y \le d < T_{y+1}$ and $\rowof(d) = d - T_{\colof(d)}$. The witness of \eqref{eq:rs} is
the embedding of $q$ into the virtual word domain
($\ell = n + k$, $e = \rowof + 2^n\colof$), zero elsewhere.
Accordingly we write the claim's point as
$z = (z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm c})$ with $z_{\mathrm r} \in \F^{n}$
and $z_{\mathrm c} \in \F^{k}$; since $\eq$ factors over any
coordinate partition,
$\eq(z, e) = \eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,
\eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))$ at the in-stack address $e$ of dense
index $d$. The jagged
multilinear
\[
  \fjag(z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm c}, \rho)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{d \in \{0,1\}^m}
    \eq(\rho, d)\,
    \eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,
    \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))
\]
admits a succinct delegated evaluation (the \emph{assist}
\cite{jagged}): a sumcheck over the $2(m{+}1)$ bits of the boundary
pairs $(T_{y-1}, T_y)$ against a width-$4$ read-once branching program,
with a transparent boundary-indicator weight; verifier cost
$O(\mathrm{cols}\cdot m)$, proof size $2(m{+}1)$ degree-$2$ rounds.

In the standard pipeline, \eqref{eq:rs} is discharged by a product
sumcheck over the \emph{virtual} domain, and the resulting evaluation
claim is transported to the dense commitment by a separate jagged
argument. The virtual-domain sumcheck costs the prover
$\Theta(2^{\ell})$ evaluations of $\Phi$ and the dense fold chain of
$b$, independently of the committed size $2^m$ --- the object of this
note is to remove
that cost.

\section{The merged reduction}\label{sec:merged}

\begin{lemma}[restriction]\label{lem:restriction}
Let $f$ be the embedding of $q$, with $z_{\mathrm r} \in \F^{n}$ and
$z_{\mathrm c} \in \F^{k}$ the claim's row and column points. Define
$W : \{0,1\}^m \to \F$ by
\[
  W[d] = \Phi\bigl(\eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\cdot
                   \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))\bigr).
\]
Then $\sum_{e} f(e)\, b(e) = \sum_{d} q[d]\, W[d]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map $d \mapsto e(d) := \rowof(d) + 2^n \colof(d)$ is a bijection
from $\{0,1\}^m$ onto the in-stack addresses, with $f(e(d)) = q[d]$ and
$f = 0$ at every other address. Hence
\[
  \sum_{e} f(e)\, b(e)
  \;=\; \sum_{d} q[d]\; b\bigl(e(d)\bigr)
  \;=\; \sum_{d} q[d]\;
        \Phi\bigl(\eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,
                  \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))\bigr)
  \;=\; \sum_{d} q[d]\, W[d],
\]
the middle step by the factorization
$\eq(z, e(d)) = \eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,
\eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))$ of \S\ref{sec:setting}.
\end{proof}

The \emph{merged sumcheck} is the product sumcheck for
$\sum_d q[d]\,W[d] = t$ over the dense cube: $m$ rounds, prover cost
one $\Phi$-evaluation per \emph{dense} word (a count-proportional
pass, fused with the first round message) plus standard folds. It is simultaneously ring switching's sumcheck and the
dense/virtual transport: its output claim
$c^* = \hat q(\rho)\,\widehat W(\rho)$ needs only an \emph{ordinary}
eq-basis opening of the commitment for $\hat q(\rho)$ --- the
special-purpose transport machinery retires --- and one new verifier
obligation: $\widehat W(\rho)$, the MLE over dense coordinates of the
\emph{$\Phi$-twisted} jagged weight. Untwisted, this is exactly the
assist's object; twisted, the assist does not apply, because a sumcheck
can only bind variables entering the summand $\F$-linearly and
$\Phi(cx) \ne c\,\Phi(x)$.

\section{The Frobenius decomposition}\label{sec:frobenius}

Every $\Ftwo$-linear $L : \F \to \F$ is uniquely a linearized
polynomial $L(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{127} c_j\, x^{2^j}$ \cite{ore,lidl}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:transport}
For Boolean $\beta$ and any $z \in \F$,
$\eq(z, \beta)^{2^j} = \eq(z^{2^j}, \beta)$. Consequently, for every
$j$,
\[
  \sum_{d \in \{0,1\}^m} \eq(\rho, d)\,
  \bigl(\eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,\eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))\bigr)^{2^j}
  \;=\;
  \fjag\bigl(z_{\mathrm r}^{2^j}, z_{\mathrm c}^{2^j}, \rho\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\eq(z,\beta) \in \{z, 1{+}z\}$ and $(1{+}z)^{2^j} = 1 + z^{2^j}$ in
characteristic $2$. Frobenius is a field automorphism, so it
distributes over the eq products, powering the $z$-coordinates and
fixing the Boolean selectors; the outer combination with the
\emph{unpowered} weights $\eq(\rho, d)$ is the definition of $\fjag$
at the powered points.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
With $\Phi(x) = \sum_j c_j\, x^{2^j}$,
\[
  \widehat W(\rho)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{j=0}^{127} c_j\;
  \fjag\bigl(z_{\mathrm r}^{2^j},\, z_{\mathrm c}^{2^j},\, \rho\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the linearized form per term of $\widehat W(\rho) = \sum_d
\eq(\rho,d) W[d]$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} per power.
\end{proof}

The twisted evaluation is thus a known $\F$-combination of $128$
\emph{ordinary} assist statements, differing from the implemented ones
only in their points. The obstruction never
arises: $\Phi$ is decomposed into automorphisms that pass through the
product structure, so no $\Phi$-interior variable is ever bound.

\begin{remark}[coefficients]\label{rem:moore}
The $c_j$ are deterministic transcript functions: evaluating $\Phi$
on the bit basis gives a linear system whose Moore matrix
$M[t][j] = e_t^{2^j}$ is universal and precomputed once; per proof,
$128$ evaluations of $\Phi$ plus one $128^2$-multiply matvec.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[dual form: a multipoint view]\label{rem:dual}
The twists can be moved off the claim points and onto $\rho$. Writing
$a_d := \eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,\eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))$,
the $j$-th statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
$\sum_d \eq(\rho, d)\, a_d^{2^j}$; since squaring is additive in
characteristic $2$ and $\eq(\sigma, d)^2 = \eq(\sigma^2, d)$ at
Boolean $d$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} in the $\rho$-slot),
\[
  \textstyle\sum_d \eq(\rho, d)\, a_d^{2}
  \;=\;
  \bigl(\sum_d \eq(\rho^{2^{-1}}, d)\, a_d\bigr)^{2},
\]
and iterating,
\[
  \fjag\bigl(z_{\mathrm r}^{2^j}, z_{\mathrm c}^{2^j}, \rho\bigr)
  \;=\;
  \fjag\bigl(z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm c}, \rho^{2^{-j}}\bigr)^{2^j},
\]
where $\rho^{2^{-j}}$ is the coordinate-wise $j$-fold Frobenius
inverse (iterated square roots --- cheap linear maps). In this form
all $128$ statements evaluate the \emph{single untwisted} jagged
multilinear at $128$ derived points: the batched assist is a
multipoint-evaluation argument for $\fjag(z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm c},
\cdot)$, and every $z$-side quantity in the assist (column weights,
the $z$-half of each transition weight) becomes statement-independent.
No formulation collapses the family further: expanding the twisted
factors over an $\Ftwo$-basis writes the $128$ statement values as the
image, under the invertible Moore matrix, of $128$ independent
bilinear contractions of the $\rho$-side data --- the batch width is
information-inherent, and only the surrounding structure (points,
coefficients, boundary combinatorics, verifier chains) can be shared.
\end{remark}

\section{The batched assist}\label{sec:protocol}

The $128$ statements share the boundary-bit domain and batch into
\emph{one} sumcheck: the prover sends $V$ (claimed
$\widehat W(\rho)$) and both parties run the $2(m{+}1)$-round sumcheck
for
\[
  \sum_{(u,v)} H(u,v) = V,
  \qquad
  H(u,v) = \sum_{j} c_{j}\; U_{j}(u,v)\; \hat g_{j}(u,v),
\]
with $U_{j}, \hat g_{j}$ the assist's boundary weight and
branching-program value at the powered points. Two observations:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Deterministic weights.} Only the combined scalar $V$ is
  used downstream, so no batching randomness is needed: $H$ has degree
  $\le 2$ per variable and plain sumcheck soundness on $H$ suffices.
  Proof size is that of a single assist ($2(m{+}1)$ rounds),
  independent of the batch width.
\item \emph{Frobenius chains.} At the final point $\sigma$ the verifier
  evaluates $H(\sigma)$ itself: the boundary factor is
  $j$-independent and computed once, and
  $\eq(z_{\mathrm c}^{2^j}, y) = \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, y)^{2^j}$ is one
  squaring per column per $j$ --- the verifier stays column-linear at
  a small constant ($\approx 6\times$ the untwisted assist path), plus
  $128$ width-$4$ program evaluations of $O(m)$ each and the Moore
  extraction of Remark~\ref{rem:moore}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Protocol.} After ring switching (unchanged): (1) the merged
sumcheck ($m$ rounds) ending at $\rho$ with running claim $c^*$;
(2) the prover sends $V$; (3) the batched assist ($2(m{+}1)$ rounds)
for $\sum H = V$; (4) the verifier checks
$c^* = \hat q(\rho)\cdot V$, with $\hat q(\rho)$ discharged by the
ordinary eq-basis opening.

\begin{theorem}[soundness, sketch]\label{thm:soundness}
Against the committed $q$: if the ring-switching statement is false
for the embedding of $q$, the verifier accepts with
probability at most
$(2m + 4(m{+}1))/|\F| + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{open}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:restriction} the merged statement equals the
ring-switching statement for the embedding. If it is false, sumcheck
soundness catches the merged rounds unless the final relation is false
for the honest $\widehat W(\rho)$; the opening binds $\hat q(\rho)$,
and the assist sumcheck binds $V$ to the honest $\widehat W(\rho)$ ---
by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, $H$'s true sum is $\widehat W(\rho)$, all
of $c_{j}, U_{j}, \hat g_{j}$ being transcript-determined objects the
verifier evaluates itself. Union-bound the two sumchecks.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[multiple claims]\label{rem:multi}
$K$ ring-switched claims, each with its own point $z_i$ and fold map
$\Phi_i$, batch additively with scalars absorbed into the $\Phi_i$ (a
scalar multiple of an $\Ftwo$-linear map is again $\Ftwo$-linear):
$b = \sum_i \Phi_i(\eq(z_i, \cdot))$, $t = \sum_i t_i$. Everything
above extends coordinate-wise: $W$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gain a
sum over $i$, the assist's summand becomes
$\sum_{i,j} c_{i,j}\, U_{i,j}\, \hat g_{i,j}$ --- still one sumcheck
of the same length --- and the Moore extraction runs once per claim.
Our instantiation has $K = 2$ (the two evaluation claims a PIOP leaves
on the committed witness), hence $256$ statements in one sumcheck.
\end{remark}

\section{Scaling in the number of columns: a multipoint
protocol}\label{sec:multipoint}

The batched assist walks the boundary program once per statement:
prover time $O(128\,K \cdot \mathrm{cols} \cdot m)$ and, for the
round streaming, suffix state of the same size. At hundreds of columns
this is a few milliseconds; a registry with tens of thousands of
columns pays hundreds of milliseconds and gigabytes. The
$128\times$ factor is \emph{not} inherent to the verifier's goal: by
Remark~\ref{rem:dual} the statements are evaluations of the single
untwisted $\fjag$, and only their $c$-combination $V$ is consumed. The
missing piece is an asymmetry:

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:twisted-eq}
Fix $\rho$ and weights $\gamma_j \in \F$, and let
$g : \{0,1\}^m \to \F$, $g_d = \sum_j \gamma_j\,
\eq(\rho, d)^{2^{-j}}$. Then for every $x \in \F^m$,
\[
  \hat g(x)
  \;=\;
  \sum_j \gamma_j \prod_{i=1}^{m}
  \Bigl(x_i\,\rho_i^{2^{-j}} + (1{-}x_i)\bigl(1{-}\rho_i^{2^{-j}}\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\eq(\rho,d)^{2^{-j}} = \eq(\rho^{2^{-j}}, d)$ at Boolean $d$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} in the $\rho$-slot, inverted), and
$\sum_{d} \eq(x,d)\,\eq(y,d) = \prod_i (x_i y_i +
(1{-}x_i)(1{-}y_i))$ coordinate-wise.
\end{proof}

The twisted \emph{jagged} weight has no such closed form --- that is
the whole subject of this note --- but the twisted \emph{eq} vector
collapses. This yields a protocol whose only per-column proof work is
one untwisted assist. For clarity take $K = 1$ and all $c_j \ne 0$;
write $a_d = \eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,\eq(z_{\mathrm c},
\colof(d))$ for the untwisted weight vector.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The prover sends $A_j := \fjag(z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm c},
  \rho^{2^{-j}})$ for $j = 0, \dots, 127$. The verifier computes
  $V = \sum_j c_j A_j^{2^j}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:dual}) and uses it as
  $\widehat W(\rho)$ in the product check.
\item The verifier sends $\gamma$; both parties form the claim
  $\sum_d a_d\, g_d = \sum_j \gamma^j A_j$ with
  $g_d = \sum_j \gamma^j \eq(\rho,d)^{2^{-j}}$. The map $x \mapsto
  \sum_j \gamma^j x^{2^{-j}}$ is $\Ftwo$-linear, so the prover
  materializes $g$ in one byte-table pass over the dense domain; $a$
  is one further dense pass (no fold map at all).
\item One ordinary $m$-round product sumcheck for this claim, ending
  at $\rho''$ with running claim $\hat a(\rho'')\,\hat g(\rho'')$.
\item The verifier evaluates $\hat g(\rho'')$ by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:twisted-eq} ($O(128\,m)$ multiplies) and discharges
  $\hat a(\rho'') = \fjag(z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm c}, \rho'')$ with
  \emph{one} untwisted assist.
\end{enumerate}

Soundness composes from standard pieces: $\gamma$ arrives after the
$A_j$, so a false value falsifies the $\gamma$-combination except with
probability $127/|\F|$ (a nonzero polynomial in $\gamma$); the
sumcheck then binds the combination to the honest $\hat a$, anchored
by the assist at $\rho''$ --- $a$ itself is never committed, the
assist playing the virtual-opening role. $K$ claims run in the same
sumcheck: with value weights $\gamma^{128 i + j}$, the per-claim
weight vectors coincide up to the scalar $\gamma^{128 i}$, so the
combined summand is $(\sum_i \gamma^{128 i} a_{i,d})\cdot g_d$ ---
still one product of two dense vectors --- and the anchor is one
$K$-statement untwisted assist with deterministic weights
$\gamma^{128 i}$ (sound as in \S\ref{sec:protocol}: only the combined
scalar is consumed).

Prover cost: two dense passes, one $m$-round sumcheck, one untwisted
assist, plus \emph{computing} the $128 K$ values --- a pure
computation with no protocol coupling, $O(128\,K \cdot \mathrm{cols}
\cdot m)$ naively and open to per-column incremental evaluation along
the boundary stride. The suffix-state footprint of the batched assist
disappears entirely. At small column counts the batched assist of
\S\ref{sec:protocol} remains cheaper (the dense passes dominate);
the crossover is on the order of a thousand columns, past which this
protocol's proof work is column-independent except for the single
assist.

For the value computation itself, runs of \emph{equal} heights ---
the registry's actual shape: per type, $k_t$ consecutive columns of
height $n_t$, any integer --- admit an evaluation far below the
generic bound. The column value is the matrix product
$e_I^{\!\top} M_0 \cdots M_m\, e_S$, splittable at any layer $\ell$
into a low row-vector times the high suffix; within a run of stride
$h$, the pair's low-$\ell$ bits are a function of $T_y \bmod 2^\ell$
alone, so one table of $2^\ell$ row-vectors (doubling build,
$16 \cdot 2^\ell$ multiplies, $\ell \approx \log h + 2$) serves the
whole run, the high suffix changes rarely (amortized free), and each
column costs one $4$-multiply dot --- $O(1)$ per column after an
$O(h)$ table, a win whenever $k \gtrsim h/\log h$, chosen adaptively
per run against the incremental fallback (recompute from the highest
changed boundary bit, amortized $O(\log h)$ layers).

A prototype confirms all of it ($m = 23$, $K = 2$, Apple silicon;
exact-value tests against the brute-force twisted weight, including
non-power-of-two strided shapes): prove totals $17 / 24 / 31$\,ms
across $368 / 4096 / 32768$ columns --- and $29$\,ms at $27962$
columns of height $300$, the non-power-of-two stride --- of which the
dense passes and sumcheck hold flat at ${\approx}15$\,ms, the values
are $2 / 7.5 / 8.9$\,ms, and the single anchor assist is
$0.4 / 1.7 / 7$\,ms (column-parallelized past ${\sim}2^{14}$ columns;
the largest remaining column-linear term, and the run tabulation
applies to it in principle). Verification measures
$0.3 / 0.6 / 1.7$\,ms. The batched assist measures $5.5$\,ms at $368$
columns and is memory-infeasible past a few thousand.

\section{Implementation}\label{sec:eval}

The construction is implemented in the Flock proof system (batch-hash
proving over $\F_{2^{128}}$, jagged integer-lane Ligerito commitment
\cite{ligero,ligerito}), side by side with the standard pipeline.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the coefficient extraction, and the batched
assist are each pinned by exact tests against brute-force oracles
(random non-power-of-two heights, zero-height columns, powers through
$j = 40$); the full pipeline round-trips with a tamper matrix on real
mixed instances. At a production-scale workload ($2^{30}$-bit virtual
domain at the right-sized capacity tier), the standard pipeline's
ring-switching stage measures $12.3 / 23.0 / 44.7$\,ms at
$100\%/50\%/25\%$ utilization --- linear in the virtual domain ---
while the merged pipeline's corresponding stages
($\Phi$-pass ${\approx}4$\,ms, merged sumcheck ${\approx}4$\,ms,
batched assist ${\approx}7$\,ms at $K = 2$) are tier-independent: prove-time
growth from the right-sized to the $4\times$ tier is $+14$\,ms versus
$+41$\,ms, with right-sized totals at parity and the standard
pipeline's multi-hundred-MB weight transient eliminated. Verifier
overhead is below $1$\,ms at the deployed layout.

\begin{remark}
The technique needs only an $\Ftwo$-linear per-word fold map, an
untwisted weight with a transparent-layer delegated evaluation, and
cheap Frobenius; it should transfer to other small-field systems
composing packing with shape-flexible commitments. Open for the full
version: the complete soundness composition (transcript schedule,
extractability of the underlying components) and a precise statement
of the deterministic-weight batching boundary.
\end{remark}
