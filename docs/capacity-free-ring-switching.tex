\begin{abstract}
Small-field SNARKs commit to $\Ftwo$-valued witnesses packed into an
extension field, and discharge bit-level evaluation claims against the
packed commitment by \emph{ring switching}. Sparse and dynamically-shaped
workloads are served by \emph{jagged} (stacked-column) commitments, which
commit only the declared data of a large virtual address space. Composing
the two creates a cost anomaly: ring switching's weight, and the sumcheck
that discharges it, live in the \emph{virtual} (capacity-padded)
coordinates, so the prover pays one $\Ftwo$-linear table-fold evaluation
per \emph{virtual} word --- a cost proportional to provisioned capacity
rather than committed data. We first show this floor is inherent to the
standard pipeline: a backward-closure argument proves every entry of the
ring-switching weight is load-bearing, so no support-skipping scheme can
beat it. We then remove the floor by \emph{merging} the two reductions:
one sumcheck over the dense domain, whose weight is the jagged transport
weight composed with the ring-switching fold map $\Phi$ --- making the
prover's pass count-proportional. The verifier's new obligation, a
succinct evaluation of this \emph{$\Phi$-twisted} jagged multilinear, is
resolved by a change of basis for $\Phi$ itself: every $\Ftwo$-linear map
on $\Fbig$ is a linearized polynomial $\sum_j c_j x^{2^j}$, and Frobenius
commutes with the eq-product structure at Boolean points, so the twisted
evaluation is a known linear combination of $128$ \emph{ordinary} jagged
evaluations at Frobenius-powered points --- delegated by one batched
sumcheck of the existing ``assist'' form, with deterministic weights and
no new batching randomness. A prototype in the Flock proof system
validates the construction end to end: transport cost becomes
near-capacity-free (prove-time growth $+14$\,ms versus $+41$\,ms for the
baseline from a right-sized tier to $4\times$ over-capacity at a
production-scale workload), at parity with the shipped transport at the
right-sized tier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Proof systems over small fields commit to witnesses whose entries are
bits, packed $128$ per word into an extension field
$\F = \Fbig$~\cite{binius,dp-ringswitch}. The arithmetization speaks about
the \emph{bit-level} multilinear $\hat z$; the commitment binds the
\emph{packed} multilinear $\hat f$. \emph{Ring switching}
\cite{dp-ringswitch} bridges the two: it reduces a bit-level evaluation
claim to an inner-product claim $\langle f, b\rangle = t$ against a
transparent weight $b$, where per word
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rsweight}
  b[e] \;=\; \Phi\bigl(\eq(z, e)\bigr),
\end{equation}
$z$ is the claim's word-level point and $\Phi : \F \to \F$ is an
$\Ftwo$-linear ``fold'' map assembled from the claim's in-word structure
and fresh verifier randomness. Crucially, $b$ does \emph{not} factor as
the eq tensor of any point: $\Phi$'s per-entry bit surgery does not
commute with field multiplication.

Independently, systems serving heterogeneous or dynamically-sized
workloads commit \emph{jaggedly}~\cite{jagged}: the committed object is a
dense stack $q$ of the declared columns' declared rows, while the
arithmetization addresses a large \emph{virtual} space --- a
capacity-sized grid in which undeclared rows, unused columns, and
alignment gaps exist only as zeros. A transport argument carries claims
across the dense/virtual translation; because the translation's
branching-program machinery consumes eq-shaped (tensor-factoring) claims,
the natural pipeline runs ring switching \emph{first}, on the virtual
domain, and transports a single plain evaluation:
\[
  \text{bit claims}
  \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{ring switch}\ }\;
  \langle f, b\rangle \text{ on the \emph{virtual} word domain}
  \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{sumcheck}\ }\;
  \hat f(\rho)
  \;\xrightarrow{\ \text{jagged transport}\ }\;
  \langle q, W_\rho\rangle .
\]

\paragraph{The capacity tax.} This composition has a cost anomaly. Let
$2^\ell$ be the virtual word domain (provisioned capacity) and
$N \le 2^\ell$ the committed size. Every stage of a well-engineered
pipeline can be made count-proportional --- witness generation, the PIOP's
sumchecks, the commitment itself, the transport --- \emph{except} the
ring-switching stage: its weight \eqref{eq:rsweight} is supported on the
whole virtual domain, and the sumcheck discharging
$\langle f, b\rangle$ runs there. The prover pays $\Theta(2^\ell)$
evaluations of $\Phi$ (in practice, a $16{\times}256$-entry byte-table
fold per word per claim) plus the dense fold chain of $b$ --- regardless
of $N$. In our measurements on a production-scale workload
(\S\ref{sec:eval}), this stage is by a wide margin the dominant cost of
provisioning capacity headroom: at $4\times$ over-capacity it accounts
for $+32$\,ms of a $+40$\,ms total prove-time growth.

One might hope to make the stage support-proportional, as the other
stages are. Our first result is negative: within the standard pipeline
the floor is inherent.

\begin{proposition}[informal; see \S\ref{sec:floor}]
In the sumcheck for $\langle f, b\rangle$ over the virtual domain, every
entry of every round's folded $b$-array is consumed by a later round
message on any witness whose folded support eventually covers the domain
(which holds for every non-degenerate jagged layout). Consequently any
prover strategy evaluates the weight on the full virtual domain:
$\Omega(2^\ell)$ evaluations of $\Phi$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{This work.} We remove the floor by \emph{merging} the two
reductions. Since $f$ vanishes off the committed stack, the type
converter's sum restricts exactly to the dense domain:
\[
  \sum_{x \in \{0,1\}^\ell} f(x)\, b(x)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{d \in \{0,1\}^{m}} q[d]\; W[d],
  \qquad
  W[d] \;=\; \Phi\bigl(\eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d)) \cdot
                        \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))\bigr),
\]
where $2^m \approx N$ is the dense domain and
$(\rowof, \colof)$ is the jagged unrank. One sumcheck over the dense
domain is then \emph{simultaneously} ring switching's sumcheck and the
dense/virtual translation; the prover's $\Phi$-pass becomes
count-proportional, and the downstream opening degenerates to an ordinary
eq-basis opening of $\hat q(\rho)$ --- retiring the transport's
special-purpose machinery (in our implementation: the fused transport
basis, a prover-sent evaluation vector, and its spot-check binding).

The new problem is the verifier's: at the sumcheck's final point $\rho$
it must evaluate $\widehat W(\rho)$, the multilinear extension over
\emph{dense} coordinates of the \emph{$\Phi$-twisted} jagged weight.
Untwisted ($\Phi = \mathrm{id}$), this is precisely the object that
jagged transports already delegate succinctly (the ``assist'': a
sumcheck against a width-$4$ read-once branching program over the
column-boundary bits \cite{jagged}). Twisted, every standard tool fails,
and for one root cause: $\Phi$ is $\Ftwo$-linear but not $\F$-linear
($\Phi(cx) \ne c\,\Phi(x)$), so it passes through neither the branching
program's multiplicative transitions nor a sumcheck's variable bindings.

Our main technical contribution resolves this by a change of basis for
$\Phi$ itself:

\begin{theorem}[informal; Theorem~\ref{thm:frobenius}]
Write $\Phi(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{127} c_j\, x^{2^j}$ (the linearized-polynomial
form of an $\Ftwo$-linear map on $\Fbig$). Then
\[
  \widehat W(\rho)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{j=0}^{127} c_j\;
  \hat f_{\mathrm{jag}}\bigl(z_{\mathrm r}^{2^j},\;
                             z_{\mathrm c}^{2^j},\; \rho\bigr),
\]
where $\hat f_{\mathrm{jag}}$ is the ordinary (untwisted) jagged
multilinear and $z^{2^j}$ denotes the coordinate-wise Frobenius power.
The point $\rho$ is not powered.
\end{theorem}

The proof is two lines once set up: Frobenius is a field automorphism,
and at a Boolean selector $\eq(z, \beta)^{2^j} = \eq(z^{2^j}, \beta)$, so
each Frobenius power of the twisted weight \emph{is} the untwisted weight
at powered points. The twisted evaluation is therefore a known
$\F$-linear combination of $128$ ordinary assist statements, and the
$c_j$ are computable by both parties from the transcript via a universal
precomputed inverse Moore matrix. The $128K$ statements ($K$ = number of
ring-switched claims; $K = 2$ in our system) batch into \emph{one}
sumcheck of the existing assist form with \textbf{deterministic} weights:
since only the combined scalar is ever used, no fresh batching randomness
is required, and plain sumcheck soundness on the combined summand
suffices. Proof size is unchanged (rounds $=$ variables, independent of
the batch width); the verifier's work stays linear in the layout's
column count with a small constant ($\approx 300$ operations per column
--- Frobenius powers of the column weights are successive squarings).

\paragraph{Secondary results.} Along the way we record: (i) the
backward-closure argument behind the floor (\S\ref{sec:floor}); (ii) a
\emph{bilinear-tensor branching program} showing the twisted object is
computable by a read-once linear program over the state
$(\text{carry},\text{comparison}) \otimes (\F \otimes_{\Ftwo} \F)$, with
$\Phi$ applied once, outside all multiplications --- yielding a
delegation-free ``fat verifier'' alternative whose cost is
$\approx 25$K multiplications per column (viable for small layouts
only); and (iii) a \emph{bindability lemma} explaining precisely why
that tensor program cannot be sumcheck-delegated directly (the pairing
$B(a \otimes b) = a\,\Phi(b)$ is $\F$-linear in its first slot only), which
is what makes the Frobenius route necessary and, we believe,
canonical.

\paragraph{Implementation.} We implemented the merged reduction in the
Flock proof system (multi-hash batch proving over $\Fbig$ with a jagged
Ligerito-based commitment), side by side with the shipped pipeline. The
core identity, the coefficient extraction, and the batched assist are
each pinned by exact tests against brute-force oracles; the full
pipeline round-trips with a tamper matrix on real mixed instances. At a
production-scale workload ($2 \times 16384$ hash compressions,
$2^{30}$-bit virtual domain at the right-sized tier), transport-cost
growth from the right-sized tier to $4\times$ over-capacity is
$+14$\,ms merged versus $+41$\,ms baseline, with right-sized prove time
at parity (\S\ref{sec:eval}).

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Notation.} $\F = \Fbig$; witnesses are $\Ftwo$-valued. For
$\tau, x \in \F^m$, $\eq(\tau, x) = \prod_i (\tau_i x_i +
(1-\tau_i)(1-x_i))$; for a vector $v \in \F^{2^m}$, $\hat v$ is its
multilinear extension (MLE); index bits are little-endian. For
$z \in \F^n$ we write $z^{2^j}$ for the coordinate-wise $2^j$-th power.
All sumchecks are standard \cite{sumcheck}; a degree-$2$ round sends
$(G(1), G(\infty))$ with $G(0)$ reconstructed from the running claim.

\paragraph{Ring switching.} We use ring switching as a black box with
the following interface (cf.~\cite{dp-ringswitch} and the deployed
variant in Flock). A bit-level claim $\hat z(\zeta) = v$ over a
$2^{7+\ell}$-point bit domain is reduced, using a prover message (the
$128$-element slice fold $\hat s_v$) and fresh verifier randomness
$r'' \in \F^{7}$, to an inner-product claim
$\langle f, b \rangle = t$ over the $2^\ell$-word packed domain, with
$b[e] = \Phi(\eq(z, e))$ as in \eqref{eq:rsweight}: $z \in \F^{\ell}$ is
the word-level part of $\zeta$, and $\Phi$ is the $\Ftwo$-linear map
$x \mapsto \sum_{i : \mathrm{bit}_i(x) = 1} w_i$ for transparent weights
$w \in \F^{128}$ derived from $r''$ (with the claim's batching scalar
$\gamma$ absorbed). Multiple claims $\gamma$-combine into a single pair
$(b_{\mathrm{comb}}, t_{\mathrm{comb}})$ with
$b_{\mathrm{comb}} = \sum_i \Phi_i(\eq(z_i, \cdot))$. The verifier can
evaluate $\hat b_{\mathrm{comb}}$ at any point in polylogarithmic time
(the standard tensor-algebra closed form); it is the \emph{prover-side}
bulk handling of $b$ that concerns us.

\paragraph{Jagged layouts.} A jagged layout over a virtual grid of
$2^{n}$ rows $\times$ $2^{k}$ columns assigns each column $y$ a height
$h_y \in [0, 2^n]$, with prefix sums $T_y = \sum_{y' < y} h_{y'}$ and
$\area = T_{2^k}$. The committed object is the dense stack
$q \in \F^{2^m}$, $2^m \ge \area$, with $q[d]$ for
$d < \area$ holding the entry at $(\rowof(d), \colof(d))$ ---
$\colof(d)$ the unique $y$ with $T_y \le d < T_{y+1}$ and
$\rowof(d) = d - T_{\colof(d)}$ --- and $q[d] = 0$ for $d \ge \area$.
The virtual word domain has $2^{\ell}$ points, $\ell = n + k$, with word
address $e = \rowof + 2^n \colof$; the embedding
$f = \mathrm{embed}(q)$ places $q$'s entries at their virtual addresses
and is zero elsewhere (heights, hence the layout, are public). The
\emph{jagged multilinear}
\[
  \hat f_{\mathrm{jag}}(z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm c}, z_{\mathrm i})
  \;=\;
  \sum_{d = 0}^{\area - 1}
    \eq(z_{\mathrm i}, d)\;
    \eq(z_{\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\;
    \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, \colof(d))
\]
is the transport's central object; the \emph{assist}~\cite{jagged}
delegates its evaluation by a sumcheck over the $2(m{+}1)$ bits of the
column-boundary pairs $(T_{y-1}, T_y)$, against a width-$4$ read-once
branching program (states: a comparison flag and a subtraction borrow)
whose per-layer transitions carry eq factors of
$(z_{\mathrm r}, z_{\mathrm i})$; the boundary-indicator weight
$U(u,v) = \sum_y \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, y)\,\eq(\mathrm{bits}(T_{y-1}), u)\,
\eq(\mathrm{bits}(T_y), v)$ is transparent, and the verifier's
end-of-sumcheck work is $O(\mathrm{cols} \cdot m)$ multiplications plus
one $O(m)$ branching-program evaluation. Proof size: $2(m{+}1)$
degree-$2$ rounds.

\paragraph{The baseline pipeline.} Ring switching first, jagged second:
the $\gamma$-combined inner product is discharged by a product sumcheck
over the \emph{virtual} domain (``the type converter''), whose output
$\hat f(\rho_{\mathrm{virt}})$ the jagged transport then carries to the
dense commitment, in our system fused into the Ligerito
opening~\cite{ligero,ligerito} with the jagged weight as the opening
basis. The ordering is forced: the transport's branching-program
machinery consumes claims whose weights factor as eq tensors, and $b$
does not factor.

\section{The padded-domain floor}\label{sec:floor}

Fix the baseline type converter: a product sumcheck for
$\sum_{x} f(x)\, b_{\mathrm{comb}}(x) = t_{\mathrm{comb}}$ over
$x \in \{0,1\}^{\ell}$, binding one variable per round; write $f_r, b_r$
for the arrays after $r$ rounds ($f_0 = f$, $b_0 = b_{\mathrm{comb}}$).
The witness side is sparse ($f = 0$ off the embedded stack) and standard
support-proportional techniques make the $f$-side folds and messages
touch only live intervals. The question is whether the $b$-side can be
made support-proportional too. Two natural strategies fail for
instructive reasons; the second gives the floor.

\emph{Factorization} (fold-free evaluation): if $b$ factored as an eq
tensor, folds would be point-bindings. It does not: $\Phi$'s bit
decomposition does not commute with field multiplication, and (a fact we
use positively in \S\ref{sec:frobenius}) $b$'s folded arrays have no
factored form because a fold's Lagrange weights multiply outside
$\Phi$.

\emph{Support-skipping} (compute folded $b$ only where folded $f$ is
live): sound at any single round --- a round message weights $b_r$ by
$f_r$, so dead entries multiply zeros --- but not across rounds.

\begin{proposition}[backward closure]\label{prop:closure}
For $r < \ell$ let $L_r \subseteq \{0,1\}^{\ell - r}$ be the positions
where $f_r$ is not identically forced to zero (the folded support), and
let $D_r$ be the set of positions at which round $r{+}1$'s message or any
later round consumes $b_r$. If $L_{r^*} = \{0,1\}^{\ell - r^*}$ for some
$r^*$ (the folded support covers the domain), then $D_r$ is the full
domain for every $r < r^*$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$D_r \supseteq \mathrm{expand}(D_{r+1}) \cup \mathrm{pairs}(L_{r+1})$:
round $r{+}1$'s message consumes $b_{r+1}$ on the pair-closure of
$L_{r+1}$, and each consumed $b_{r+1}$ entry is a combination of its two
$b_r$ children. At $r^*$, $D_{r^*}$ is the full (small) domain; expansion
of a full domain is full; induct downward.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis holds in every non-degenerate jagged instance: folding
merges each dummy region into a live block once the fold radix exceeds
the region's extent, so the folded support of any $f$ with at least one
live entry per used column covers the domain after
$\approx \log_2(\text{row capacity})$ rounds. Hence every entry of
$b_0$ influences some consumed value, and any prover strategy performs
$\Omega(2^{\ell})$ evaluations of $\Phi$: lazily generating $b$ on the
live support and \emph{backfilling} folded entries on demand is sound
and achieves the floor exactly (each virtual word's $\Phi$-value is
computed exactly once, eagerly or under a backfill), but cannot go below
it. Capacity-freedom requires leaving the virtual domain.

\section{The merged reduction}\label{sec:merged}

\begin{lemma}[restriction]\label{lem:restriction}
Let $f = \mathrm{embed}(q)$ and, for each ring-switched claim $i$, let
$z_i = (z_{i,\mathrm r}, z_{i,\mathrm c})$ be the word-level point split
at the row/column boundary of the virtual addressing. Define
$W : \{0,1\}^{m} \to \F$ by
\[
  W[d] \;=\; \sum_i \Phi_i\bigl(
     \eq(z_{i,\mathrm r}, \rowof(d)) \cdot
     \eq(z_{i,\mathrm c}, \colof(d))\bigr)
  \quad (d < \area),
  \qquad W[d] \;=\; 0 \quad (d \ge \area).
\]
Then
$\sum_{x \in \{0,1\}^{\ell}} f(x)\, b_{\mathrm{comb}}(x)
 = \sum_{d \in \{0,1\}^{m}} q[d]\, W[d]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Termwise. Off-stack virtual words contribute zero on the left ($f = 0$);
in-stack words correspond bijectively to $d < \area$ with
$\eq(z_i, e) = \eq(z_{i,\mathrm r}, \rowof(d))\,
\eq(z_{i,\mathrm c}, \colof(d))$ (the virtual address is the
concatenation of row and column indices); the dense tail $d \ge \area$
contributes zero on the right ($q$'s committed tail is zero, and $W$'s
zero-extension makes the term vanish regardless).
\end{proof}

The \emph{merged sumcheck} is the product sumcheck for
$\sum_d q[d]\, W[d] = t_{\mathrm{comb}}$ over the \emph{dense} cube
$\{0,1\}^m$. The prover materializes $W$ in one count-proportional pass
--- the layout cursor walk with one $\Phi$ table-fold per dense word per
claim --- and runs $m$ standard rounds; the zero-extension is exactly
what the assist's branching program computes past the area (its
comparison state rejects), so prover table and verifier evaluation agree
by construction. The output is a claim
$c^* = \hat q(\rho) \cdot \widehat W(\rho)$ at the final point $\rho$:
$\hat q(\rho)$ is discharged by an \emph{ordinary eq-basis} opening of
the commitment (the special-purpose transport basis and its binding
machinery retire), and $\widehat W(\rho)$ is the verifier's one new
obligation.

\begin{remark}
Soundness composes exactly as in the baseline: the commitment binds $q$;
the definitional object of all upstream claims is
$\mathrm{unpack}(\mathrm{embed}(q))$, as it already is in the baseline's
fused transport; the merged sumcheck plus the opening prove
$\langle q, W\rangle = t_{\mathrm{comb}}$ for the committed $q$, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:restriction} is the ring-switching statement for
$\mathrm{embed}(q)$. Two plain sumchecks and one opening; no new
random-oracle surface (\S\ref{sec:protocol}).
\end{remark}

\section{Evaluating the twisted weight}\label{sec:frobenius}

The verifier must evaluate
$\widehat W(\rho) = \sum_{d} \eq(\rho, d)\, W[d]$ succinctly.

\subsection{Why existing tools fail, and a structural detour}

Write each term as $\alpha(d) \cdot \Phi(\beta(d))$ with
$\alpha(d) = \eq(\rho, d)$ (all $\F$-side weights) and $\beta(d)$ the
row/column eq product inside $\Phi$ (we suppress the claim index; sums
over claims are implicit). The assist's branching program computes
$\sum \alpha \beta$ by multiplicative per-layer transitions; $\Phi$
passes through none of them. Bit-decomposing $\Phi$ fails because bit
slices of field products are not multilinear. A prover-sent value cannot
be spot-checked without an independent oracle for the same twisted
object.

The twist does linearize at the price of state width. The pairing
$B(a \otimes b) := a \cdot \Phi(b)$ is $\Ftwo$-bilinear, so
$\widehat W(\rho) = B\bigl(\sum_d \alpha(d) \otimes \beta(d)\bigr)$, and
the tensor $T = \sum_d \alpha \otimes \beta \in \F \otimes_{\Ftwo} \F$
evolves under a \emph{read-once linear} branching program: each index
bit contributes the transparent Kronecker map
$M_{u(0)} \otimes M_{v(0)} + M_{u(1)} \otimes M_{v(1)}$
(multiplication-by-constant maps are $\Ftwo$-linear), composed with the
jagged carry/comparison structure; $\Phi$ is applied once, to the final
state, outside every multiplication. Evaluated directly, per column and
layer the transitions cost a few hundred multiplications --- a
``fat verifier'' at $\approx 25$K multiplications per column, viable for
small layouts, with no new argument at all. But it does not delegate:

\begin{lemma}[bindability]\label{lem:bindability}
$B\bigl((M_x \otimes I)\, T\bigr) = x \cdot B(T)$ for all $x \in \F$,
whereas $x \mapsto B\bigl((I \otimes M_x)\, T\bigr)$ is not in general
$\F$-linear. Consequently a sumcheck can bind a variable of the tensor
program only if the variable's factors act on the $\alpha$ slot; the
row-side factors and the boundary bits act on the $\beta$ slot, so the
assist's boundary-bit sumcheck does not extend to the tensor program.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$B((M_x \otimes I)(a \otimes b)) = (xa)\Phi(b) = x\,B(a \otimes b)$ and
extend linearly. For the second claim take $T = a \otimes b$ with
$\Phi(xb) \ne x\,\Phi(b)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Frobenius decomposition}

The resolution changes basis for $\Phi$ rather than fighting the
program structure. Recall two classical facts about
$\F = \F_{2^{128}}$: the Frobenius maps $x \mapsto x^{2^j}$ are field
automorphisms fixing $\Ftwo$, and every $\Ftwo$-linear
$L : \F \to \F$ is uniquely a \emph{linearized polynomial}
$L(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{127} c_j\, x^{2^j}$ \cite{ore,lidl}.

\begin{lemma}[Frobenius transport]\label{lem:transport}
For Boolean $\beta \in \{0,1\}$ and any $z \in \F$:
$\eq(z, \beta)^{2^j} = \eq(z^{2^j}, \beta)$. Consequently, for Boolean
selector vectors and any $j$,
\[
  \sum_{d} \eq(\rho, d)\;\beta(d)^{2^j}
  \;=\;
  \hat f_{\mathrm{jag}}\bigl(z_{\mathrm r}^{2^j},\,
                             z_{\mathrm c}^{2^j},\, \rho\bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\eq(z, \beta) \in \{z,\, 1+z\}$ and $(1+z)^{2^j} = 1 + z^{2^j}$ in
characteristic $2$; Frobenius is an automorphism, so it distributes over
the products forming $\beta(d)$, powering each $z$-coordinate and fixing
the Boolean selectors; the $\F$-linear combination with the
\emph{unpowered} weights $\eq(\rho, d)$ is then the definition of
$\hat f_{\mathrm{jag}}$ at the powered points. (The carry/comparison
structure of $\rowof(d) = d - T_{\colof(d)}$ selects \emph{which}
Boolean factors appear and is untouched by the powering.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[twisted evaluation]\label{thm:frobenius}
Let $\Phi_i(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{127} c_{i,j}\, x^{2^j}$. Then
\[
  \widehat W(\rho)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{i} \sum_{j=0}^{127} c_{i,j}\;
  \hat f_{\mathrm{jag}}\bigl(z_{i,\mathrm r}^{2^j},\,
                             z_{i,\mathrm c}^{2^j},\, \rho\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the linearized form per term and Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} per
power; sums commute.
\end{proof}

No $\Phi$-interior variable is ever bound: $\Phi$ has been decomposed
into automorphisms that pass \emph{through} the product structure, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:bindability}'s obstruction never arises. The twisted
evaluation is an explicit $\F$-combination of $128K$ \emph{ordinary}
assist statements, differing from the implemented ones only in their
(Frobenius-powered) points.

\begin{remark}[coefficient extraction]\label{rem:moore}
$c_{i,j}$ is a deterministic function of transcript values: evaluating
$\Phi_i$ on the bit basis $(e_t)$ gives the linear system
$\Phi_i(e_t) = \sum_j c_{i,j}\, e_t^{2^j}$, whose Moore matrix
$M[t][j] = e_t^{2^j}$ is invertible and \emph{universal} --- computed
and inverted once, ever. Per proof: $128$ byte-table evaluations of
$\Phi_i$ plus one $128^2$-multiplication matrix--vector product per
claim.
\end{remark}

\section{The batched Frobenius assist, and the full protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{The batched assist.} The $128K$ statements share the
boundary-bit domain, so they combine into \emph{one} sumcheck for
\[
  \sum_{(u,v) \in \{0,1\}^{2(m+1)}} H(u,v) \;=\; V,
  \qquad
  H(u,v) \;=\; \sum_{i,j} c_{i,j}\; U_{i,j}(u,v)\; \hat g_{i,j}(u,v),
\]
where $U_{i,j}$ and $\hat g_{i,j}$ are the assist's boundary weight and
branching-program value at the powered points, and $V$ is the prover's
claimed $\widehat W(\rho)$. Two observations keep this cheap:

\emph{Deterministic weights.} Only the combined scalar $V$ is ever used
downstream, so the $c_{i,j}$ weights need no fresh batching randomness:
plain sumcheck soundness on the single summand $H$ (degree $\le 2$ per
variable) suffices, and the round count --- hence proof size,
$\approx 1.7$\,KB --- is independent of the batch width.

\emph{Frobenius chains.} At the final point $\sigma$ the verifier
evaluates $H(\sigma)$ itself. The boundary factor
$E_y(\sigma) = \eq(\mathrm{bits}(T_{y-1}), \sigma_u)\,
\eq(\mathrm{bits}(T_y), \sigma_v)$ is $(i,j)$-independent and computed
once; the per-$j$ column weights obey
$\eq(z_{\mathrm c}^{2^j}, y) = \eq(z_{\mathrm c}, y)^{2^j}$ --- one
squaring per column per $j$ --- so the verifier's total stays
\emph{column-linear with constant $\approx 300$} operations per column
($\approx 6\times$ the untwisted assist path), scaling to layouts of
thousands of columns; plus $128K$ width-$4$ branching-program
evaluations of $O(m)$ each and the Moore extraction of
Remark~\ref{rem:moore}.

\paragraph{Protocol.} After ring switching (unchanged: $\hat s_v$
messages, $r''$ challenges, $\gamma$-batching, targets):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Merged sumcheck} ($m$ rounds) for
        $\sum_d q[d]\,W[d] = t_{\mathrm{comb}}$, ending at $\rho$ with
        running claim $c^*$.
  \item Prover sends $V$, claimed to be $\widehat W(\rho)$.
  \item \textbf{Batched Frobenius assist} ($2(m{+}1)$ rounds) for
        $\sum H = V$; at its final point the verifier computes
        $H(\sigma)$ as above.
  \item Verifier checks $c^* = \hat q(\rho) \cdot V$, with
        $\hat q(\rho)$ discharged by an ordinary eq-basis opening of the
        commitment (for our Ligerito instantiation: the standard opening,
        whose end-of-recursion basis evaluations are closed-form eq
        evaluations --- the fused transport basis, the prover-sent
        evaluation vector, and its spot-check reduction all retire).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[soundness, sketch]\label{thm:soundness}
Against the committed $q$, if any upstream bit-level claim is false for
$\mathrm{unpack}(\mathrm{embed}(q))$, the verifier accepts with
probability at most
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rs}} + (2m + 4(m{+}1))/|\F| +
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{open}}$, where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rs}}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{open}}$ are the ring-switching and opening errors
of the underlying components.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:restriction} the merged statement equals the
$\gamma$-batched ring-switching statement for $\mathrm{embed}(q)$. If it
is false, then by sumcheck soundness either some round of the merged
sumcheck is caught, or the final relation
$c^* = \hat q(\rho)\, \widehat W(\rho)$ is false for the honest
$\widehat W(\rho)$; the opening binds $\hat q(\rho)$, and the assist
sumcheck binds $V$ to the honest $\widehat W(\rho)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:frobenius} makes $H$'s honest sum equal
$\widehat W(\rho)$, the $c_{i,j}$, $U_{i,j}$, and $\hat g_{i,j}$ being
deterministic transcript functions the verifier evaluates itself).
Union-bound the two sumchecks' errors.
\end{proof}

\section{Implementation and evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

We implemented the construction in Flock, a batch-hash proof system over
$\Fbig$ committing with a jagged, integer-lane Ligerito scheme, as a
side-by-side alternative to the shipped transport (which remains
byte-identical; its pinned-proof fixtures pass unchanged). Every layer is
oracle-tested: the Frobenius identity against a brute-force jagged MLE
(random non-power-of-two heights, zero columns, powers through
$j = 40$); the coefficient extraction against direct evaluation; the
batched assist prover against the brute-force twisted evaluation, with
round- and value-tamper rejection; and the full pipeline (merged
sumcheck, assist, eq-basis opening, integer-lane and power-of-two
commitments) as an end-to-end roundtrip with a five-way tamper matrix on
real mixed instances.

\paragraph{Measured.} Workload: $16384$ SHA-256 $+$ $16384$ BLAKE3
compressions (the system's production benchmark scale), proved on
capacity tiers $\nu = 14/15/16$ ($100\%/50\%/25\%$ utilization; virtual
domain $2^{30}$--$2^{32}$ bits), Apple silicon, best-of-passes with arms
alternated in-process. Baseline pipeline: the ring-switching stage
(weight construction $+$ type converter) measures
$12.3 / 23.0 / 44.7$\,ms across the tiers --- linear in the virtual
domain --- while every other stage is flat; total prove
$\approx 112 \to 152$\,ms. Merged pipeline: the corresponding stages
(count-proportional $\Phi$-pass fused with the round-$0$ message
$\approx 4$\,ms, merged sumcheck $\approx 4$\,ms, batched assist
$\approx 7$\,ms) are tier-independent; measured prove-time growth from
the right-sized to the $4\times$ tier is $+14$\,ms versus the baseline's
$+41$\,ms, and in a same-run comparison at the right-sized tier the
merged pipeline matched the shipped one ($124.9$ vs.\ $130.9$\,ms on
equally-warmed arms; cold-machine baselines put the two within
$\approx 5$--$10$\,ms, the residual being the assist's fixed cost).
Verifier overhead is under a millisecond at the deployed layout
($\approx 370$ columns); the $256$--$512$\,MB transient that the
baseline materializes for the ring-switching weight at over-capacity
tiers does not exist in the merged pipeline.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When to prefer which pipeline.} At full utilization the dense
and virtual domains nearly coincide and the merged pipeline's advantage
vanishes (it pays the assist's fixed cost against the baseline's near-free
transport residuals): parity is the structural ceiling there. The
construction's value is that provisioned headroom becomes free ---
relevant whenever one capacity tier must serve varying workloads.

\paragraph{Scaling in columns.} The batched assist keeps the verifier
column-linear at a small constant, so the construction scales to
many-column layouts where the fat-verifier alternative
(\S\ref{sec:frobenius}) does not. Note that no registry-time
preprocessing shortcut exists here: the layout's prefix sums are
per-proof (count-derived), unlike, e.g., circuit matrices whose MLEs can
be committed once.

\paragraph{Generality.} The technique needs only: (i) an $\Ftwo$-linear
per-word fold map (any ring-switching-style bit-to-word bridge); (ii) a
weight whose untwisted form has a succinct delegated evaluation with
transparent per-layer structure (any assist-style branching-program
object); and (iii) a field with cheap Frobenius. It should therefore
apply to other small-field systems composing packing with
shape-flexible commitments, e.g.\ tower-field constructions where
Frobenius is likewise inexpensive. The pattern --- decompose an
$\Ftwo$-linear obstruction into linearized-polynomial form and transport
each Frobenius power through the Boolean product structure --- may be of
independent use; it is, in effect, a change of basis that converts a
non-multiplicative twist into a batch of point translations.

\paragraph{Open items.} The soundness argument here is a sketch
(Theorem~\ref{thm:soundness}); a full write-up should fix the transcript
schedule and compose with the underlying components' extractability
statements. The assist prover's constant ($128K$ small statements) has
known slack. Finally, the deterministic-weight batching deserves a
careful statement in its own right: it replaces a random linear
combination with transcript-determined weights, which is sound here
because only the combined value is consumed, but the boundary of that
argument is worth delineating.
